# Machine Translation Results

## TL;DR {#tldr}
On both WMT 2014 English-to-German and English-to-French translation benchmarks, the Transformer set a new state of the art while costing far less to train than prior architectures. This result is the empirical payoff of the Transformer's design: dropping recurrence in favor of attention doesn't just simplify the model, it also translates better and cheaper. The gains build directly on the Training setup and model architecture described elsewhere in the paper, and are corroborated by the Model Variations ablations, which show which architectural choices actually drove the improvement.

## Intuition {#intuition}
Prior best systems (ensembles of recurrent or convolutional seq2seq models) needed enormous compute to reach competitive BLEU scores. The Transformer's attention-only architecture parallelizes much better than recurrence, so it can reach higher quality using a small fraction of the training FLOPs previous state-of-the-art models required — and it does so with a single model, not an ensemble. The headline finding is simple: better translations, less compute, no ensembling tricks needed.

## Mechanics {#mechanics}
Two model sizes were evaluated: a "base" Transformer and a larger "big" Transformer, following the training and architecture setup established earlier in the paper. For inference, the models used beam search with a fixed beam size and a length penalty, with maximum output length capped at input length plus a small constant, and early termination when possible; these decoding hyperparameters were tuned on the development set [§sec_6_1]. To produce the final reported models, checkpoints were averaged: the base model averaged its last 5 checkpoints (written at 10-minute intervals), while the big model averaged its last 20 checkpoints [§sec_6_1]. For the big English-to-French model specifically, a higher dropout rate was used than in the base configuration [§sec_6_1]. Training cost was estimated by multiplying training time, number of GPUs, and an estimated sustained single-precision FLOPS per GPU, giving the FLOPS figures used to compare against prior architectures like ByteNet, GNMT+RL, ConvS2S, and MoE [§sec_6_1].

## The Math {#the-math}
The comparison metric throughout is BLEU score, reported per model per language pair alongside estimated training cost in FLOPs [§sec_6_1]. On English-to-German, the Transformer (big) achieves a new state-of-the-art BLEU score, surpassing all previously reported models and ensembles by more than 2 BLEU points, while the base model alone already exceeds all prior single models and ensembles at a fraction of their training cost [§sec_6_1]. On English-to-French, the Transformer (big) achieves a BLEU score outperforming every previously published single model, at less than 1/4 the training cost of the prior state-of-the-art [§sec_6_1]. The training-cost estimate itself is derived as time × GPU count × sustained FLOPS/GPU, which is how the paper puts the Transformer's cost figures on the same footing as ByteNet, Deep-Att+PosUnk, GNMT+RL, ConvS2S, and MoE in the results table [§sec_6_1].
