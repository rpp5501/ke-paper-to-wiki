# Causal Effects in Linear Gaussian SEMs
## TL;DR {#tldr}
This concept asks: once you fix a causal graph and its linear Gaussian parameters, what is the actual numerical effect of intervening on one variable on another? It matters for SID because SID needs to compare not just graph structure but the causal effects implied by different graphs.

## Intuition {#intuition}
In a linear Gaussian structural equation model (SEM), every variable is a weighted sum of its parents plus Gaussian noise, so the whole system's joint distribution is fully described by a covariance matrix built from the structural coefficients and noise variances. Intervening on a variable (forcing it to a value rather than letting it be generated by its usual equation) produces a new distribution over the rest of the system — and because everything is linear and Gaussian, that post-intervention distribution is still Gaussian, just with a shifted mean and unchanged variance. This lets the "causal effect" be captured as a single number (or vector) summarizing how the mean of the downstream variables moves per unit change in the intervened variable, which is exactly the kind of quantity SID algorithms need to evaluate against a candidate causal graph (prerequisite: SID Algorithms).

## Mechanics {#mechanics}
Given a linear Gaussian SEM with known structural coefficients and noise variances, the covariance matrix of all variables can be computed directly from those parameters, and for a fixed graph this same covariance matrix lets us analytically compute the causal effect of any intervention [§sec_11]. Because intervening sets the intervention target to a fixed value, the resulting distribution over the remaining variables stays Gaussian, with a mean that depends linearly on the intervention value and a variance that does not depend on it — which is why the effect can be summarized by a single "causal effect" quantity rather than a full distribution [§sec_11]. To compute this quantity, the derivation uses submatrices of the covariance matrix: one submatrix restricted to the rows and columns of the relevant variable set, and one vector taken from the row and columns corresponding to the intervention/target pair [§sec_11].
