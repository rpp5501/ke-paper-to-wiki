# Baseline Detectors (NC, TABOR, etc.)

## TL;DR {#tldr}
Baseline post-training backdoor detectors like Neural Cleanse (NC) and TABOR reverse-engineer a trigger for each candidate target class and flag classes whose trigger looks anomalously small or compact. Because this approach implicitly assumes the trigger is a small, static, patch-like pattern, these detectors work well on some backdoor pattern (BP) types but fail badly on others — a limitation the paper's UnivBD is designed to avoid by making no assumptions about BP type, number of source classes, or need for legitimate data.

## Intuition {#intuition}
Think of NC and TABOR as trying to "guess the trigger" for every possible target label by asking: what's the smallest sticker I could paste onto any image to force the model to predict this label? If one label needs a suspiciously tiny sticker compared to all the others, that label is probably backdoored. This works well when the real trigger actually is a small sticker-like patch, but it breaks down when the attacker instead poisons the whole image with a subtle blend, a warp, or a sample-specific pattern — there's no small "sticker" to find, so the detector's core assumption never gets satisfied and the attack slips through undetected.

## Mechanics {#mechanics}
NC operates by solving, independently for every class label, a gradient-based optimization that finds a minimal mask-and-pattern pair sufficient to flip any input's prediction to that label; it then applies a median-absolute-deviation (MAD) outlier test on the resulting mask L1-norms across labels, flagging a label as backdoored when its mask is anomalously small [S1]. TABOR reuses this same per-label reverse-engineering objective but augments it with extra regularization terms — covering overlay, scattered-pixel, mask-coverage, and steganalysis-inspired penalties — intended to suppress spurious or adversarial triggers and improve the fidelity of the recovered pattern [S2]. This shared architecture bakes in the presumption that the backdoor pattern (BP) is a compact, static, input-agnostic patch, which is confirmed empirically in this paper's comparison: NC shows strong detection on patch-replacement BPs (A3–A4) it is designed for, but fails to detect the local-perturbation BP (A2) [§sec_4_1_2]. Both NC and TABOR also assume the attack involves multiple source classes (the "M" setting), which makes them generally less effective against single-source-class ("S") attacks — in contrast to UnivBD, which makes no assumption on source-class count [§sec_4_1_2]. The paper further reports that TABOR does not show competitive performance relative to other methods overall, attributing this to the additional shape/color constraints it imposes on the recovered pattern [§sec_4_1_2].

Computationally, both methods require a separate multi-thousand-iteration optimization per candidate target class, so runtime scales roughly linearly with the number of classes, with TABOR costing more due to its extra loss terms [S1][S2]. This is borne out in the paper's efficiency comparison, where NC and TABOR are markedly slower than UnivBD across all four datasets — e.g. on CIFAR-100, NC takes 800s and TABOR 341s versus UnivBD's 115s [§sec_4_1_2].

## Go Deeper {#go-deeper}
- [Neural Cleanse: Identifying and Mitigating Backdoor Attacks in Neural Networks (Wang et al., IEEE S&P 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06531) — original derivation of the per-label trigger reverse-engineering + MAD outlier objective that NC baselines use.
- [TABOR: A Highly Accurate Approach to Inspecting and Restoring Trojan Backdoors in AI Systems (Guo et al.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01763) — derives TABOR's added regularization terms on top of the NC objective.
- [bolunwang/backdoor (official Neural Cleanse implementation)](https://github.com/bolunwang/backdoor) — reference code for the exact optimization/anomaly-detection pipeline used as a baseline.
- [Backdoor Learning: A Survey (Li et al.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08745) — surveys NC/TABOR alongside later detectors, useful for framing why patch-assumption baselines struggle on newer BP types.
