# Arithmetic Reasoning Results
## TL;DR {#tldr}
Chain-of-thought prompting only helps arithmetic reasoning once a model is large enough. Below roughly 100B parameters it often hurts; above that threshold, on GSM8K, SVAMP, and MAWPS it turns a losing standard-prompting baseline into new state-of-the-art results.

## Intuition {#intuition}
Think of chain-of-thought prompting as asking the model to show its work instead of blurting out an answer.

Below roughly 100B parameters, a model doesn't yet have the arithmetic and language skill to write work that is even internally consistent, so showing work mostly gives it more chances to go wrong.

Once a model is large enough to write coherent multi-step reasoning, that reasoning becomes a genuine aid, and the aid matters more the harder the problem is.

## Mechanics {#mechanics}
The paper reports results across three model families — LaMDA, GPT-3, and PaLM — each tested at several parameter scales with both standard prompting and chain-of-thought prompting on GSM8K, SVAMP, and MAWPS [§sec_3_2].

On GSM8K, the CoT-minus-standard gap flips from negative to strongly positive as scale increases, and the size at which it flips differs by model family [§sec_3_2].

| Model family | Smallest scale tested | Gap at smallest scale | Largest scale tested | Gap at largest scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LaMDA | 0.4B | standard 2.6%, CoT 0.4% (−2.2pp) [§sec_3_2] | 137B | standard 6.5%, CoT 14.3% (+7.8pp) [§sec_3_2] |
| GPT | 0.35B | standard 2.2%, CoT 0.5% (−1.7pp) [§sec_3_2] | 175B | standard 15.6%, CoT 46.9% (+31.3pp) [§sec_3_2] |
| PaLM | 8B | standard 4.9%, CoT 4.1% (−0.8pp) [§sec_3_2] | 540B | standard 17.9%, CoT 56.9% (+39.0pp) [§sec_3_2] |

Chain-of-thought prompting also helps more on harder problems within the same benchmark family: GSM8K, the benchmark with the lowest baseline accuracy, shows the largest CoT gains, while SingleOp — the one-step subset of MAWPS — shows gains that are negative or negligible [§sec_3_2].

With the largest models, chain-of-thought prompting matches or beats prior task-specific supervised systems on most benchmarks tested [§sec_3_2].

| Benchmark | Standard (540B) | CoT (540B) | Prior supervised best |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSM8K | 17.9% [§sec_3_2] | 56.9% [§sec_3_2] | 55% [§sec_3_2] |
| SVAMP | 68.6% [§sec_3_2] | 73.9% [§sec_3_2] | 47.3% [§sec_3_2] |
| MAWPS | 79.2% [§sec_3_2] | 93.3% [§sec_3_2] | 88.4% [§sec_3_2] |

On SVAMP, standard prompting alone already exceeds the prior best, so CoT's contribution there is a further gain on top of a baseline that had already surpassed the supervised system [§sec_3_2].

To understand why chain-of-thought prompting works, the authors manually inspected chains of thought generated by 137B PaLM on 100 GSM8K examples — 50 where the model's final answer was correct and 50 where it was wrong [§sec_3_2].

Among the 50 correct-answer examples, only two chains of thought were logically or mathematically flawed despite reaching the right final answer, meaning the final-answer metric almost never rewards a lucky guess over genuine reasoning here [§sec_3_2].

Among the 50 wrong-answer examples, 46% were nearly correct with only a minor slip such as a calculator error, a symbol-mapping error, or one missing step, while the other 54% contained a major error in semantic understanding or coherence [§sec_3_2].

A parallel comparison of 62B and 540B PaLM errors found that scaling to 540B fixes a large share of the one-step-missing and semantic-understanding errors that 62B makes, which is one concrete mechanism behind the scale-driven emergence pattern above [§sec_3_2].

## The Math {#the-math}
Take PaLM 540B on GSM8K: standard prompting solves 17.9% of problems and chain-of-thought prompting solves 56.9%, a difference of 39.0 percentage points and a ratio of 56.9/17.9 ≈ 3.18×, meaning CoT lets the same model solve roughly three times as many problems it was already large enough to attempt [§sec_3_2].

Set against the prior supervised best of 55%, CoT's 56.9% clears it by only 1.9 percentage points — a thin margin that shows CoT is closing a gap to task-specific fine-tuning, not blowing past it [§sec_3_2].

GPT-3 175B's GSM8K numbers show the same ratio without closing the gap: 15.6% standard to 46.9% CoT is also roughly a 3× multiplier (46.9/15.6 ≈ 3.01×), but 46.9% still falls 8.1 percentage points short of the 55% prior best [§sec_3_2].

The SVAMP comparison to prior best is confounded: standard prompting alone already scores 65.7–68.6%, well above the 47.3% prior best, so CoT's 5.2-point gain there (68.6% → 73.9% for PaLM 540B) reflects added reasoning quality, not the gap CoT is credited with closing [§sec_3_2].

The 50 correct-answer chains give an implied reliability rate of 48/50 = 96%, meaning the model's stated reasoning matched its stated answer all but 4% of the time it happened to be right — a low rate of "right answer, wrong reasoning" [§sec_3_2].

The 50 wrong-answer chains split 46%/54% between near-misses and major errors; since those two categories are the only ones reported and sum to 100%, roughly half of this model's failures on GSM8K are one small, fixable slip away from being correct [§sec_3_2].

## Go Deeper {#go-deeper}
On the other two arithmetic benchmarks in the paper, AQuA and ASDiv, chain-of-thought prompting with the largest models lands within about 2 percentage points of the state of the art, extending the pattern seen on GSM8K, SVAMP, and MAWPS to problems with different structure [§sec_3_2].

The manual error analysis is a real qualitative window into why CoT works, but it rests on 100 examples from one benchmark and one model (137B PaLM), examined by the authors themselves rather than independent raters — a useful diagnostic, not a statistically powered claim [§sec_3_2].

The two related concept pages — Correct Chain of Thought Analysis and Incorrect Chain of Thought Analysis — unpack the 48/50 and 46%/54% splits introduced here into the actual reasoning-chain examples behind each category.
